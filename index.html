Learning Game Design and Development with Free and Accessible Resources

Welcome to the Game Development Subject Guide!

This guide is designed to help young learners, educators, families, and beginning creators explore the world of game design and development. Creating games combines storytelling, art, problem-solving, programming, and creativity. Whether you are interested in making your first game, learning how games are built, or exploring careers in the game industry, this guide provides free and low-cost resources to help you get started.

Unlike many existing game development guides that are designed for university students and often require institutional access, this guide focuses on accessible resources that anyone can use. The resources selected here are free, open source, beginner-friendly, and appropriate for children and new learners.

Why this Guide was created

Game development resources are widely available online, but finding reliable, age-appropriate, and accessible information can be challenging. Many professional guides are created for college students or industry professionals and may include expensive software, paid courses, or resources restricted to university communities.

This subject guide addresses that gap by highlighting:

  • Free and open-source software (FOSS)
  • Beginner-friendly game creation tools
  • Libraries, organizations, and communities that support digital creativity

This guide supports learners who may face barriers such as cost, lack of institutional access, or limited technology resources.

Who is this Guide for?

This guide is intended for:

  • Children and teens interested in creating games
  • Parents and caregivers supporting digital learning
  • Teachers and librarians looking for STEM resources
  • Beginning game developers
  • Anyone interested in learning game design without expensive tools

No previous programming or game development experience is required.

How to use this Guide

This guide is designed to help you find reliable and accessible resources related to game development. Use the categories in the navigation menu to explore different types of information sources, including books, databases, websites, organizations, communities, and development tools.
